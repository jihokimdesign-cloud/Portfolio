01Context

Users were dropping off, but why?

TAP3D is a B2B VR training platform preparing workers for advanced manufacturing roles. Despite a seamless VR and web experience, internal data showed a significant lag in the training completion cycle — directly hurting customer retention.

Usage logs told a clear story of where trainees stalled. They couldn't tell me why.

02Research

9 interviews. 3 barriers.

To find the root cause, I ran 9 in-depth interviews with learners and performed affinity mapping on 200+ coded quotes. Two questions drove it: what makes a trainee start right now, and what's the minimum they need to commit?

Barrier 01
Commitment debt

"Feels too long." A full lecture felt like a promise trainees couldn't keep.

Solution · bite-sized checklist

The agent recommends 5–10 min actions instead of full lectures, so starting feels effortless.

Barrier 02
Decision paralysis

"I have so much to do, but I don't know what to do first."

Solution · personalized suggestion

The agent picks the best next step with a one-line reason — "Here's a 5-min safety check. Do this now."

Barrier 03
Support gap

"No quick help." Without an immediate answer, trainees deferred tasks to tomorrow.

Solution · context-aware handoff

Upload a short recording → the agent auto-drafts a message with context for the instructor. A rage-quit becomes a seamless escalation.

Make starting feel bite-sized. Make "why today" obvious. Give a quick path to help.

03Strategy

Ship fast, prove more later

I scoped three options against development cost and user value. A human mentor had the most trust but didn't scale; a full AI build was too costly to justify yet. A bite-sized, proactive checklist agent sat in the middle — low dev cost, built on LMS metadata that already existed.

05Outcome

06Reflection

What I'd carry forward

Pre-launch simulation testing wasn't common at TAP3D. This project showed its value to the C-suite and helped seed a culture of data-driven design.

01
Simulate before you build

Wizard-of-Oz turned an opinion into evidence a CFO could sign off on — before a single model was wired up.

02
Design the component, not the screen

A modular widget kept the agent consistent everywhere it appeared, and navigating PM and engineering constraints is how it got there.

Pitching the Wizard-of-Oz results to the C-suite for rollout approval.
