01Context

The 72-hour wall

LePal is an AI wellness app for Gen Z. I owned the end-to-end design for three pillars: My Journal, a mood-aware journaling flow; Crystal Ball, a daily reflective micro-interaction; and Therapy Planet, topic-driven AI chat.

This case focuses on My Journal, where the AI had to feel like a supportive companion, not a robot. The beta looked promising, but the data showed strong day-1 interest and a cliff around the 72-hour mark.

When users sounded tired or vulnerable, the AI switched into productivity mode. That empathy mismatch drove a 40% spike in session drop-offs.

EngineeringHypothesis A

"It's a memory bug."

The backend wasn't persisting context, so conversations felt disjointed.

Fix: session persistence
Design (me)Hypothesis B

"It's an empathy gap."

The AI remembered facts but failed to read feelings. Users didn't feel heard.

Fix: redesign tone and interactions
02Research

Users felt unseen

To find the why, I ran a mixed-method study with Gen Z graduate students: semi-structured interviews, a 3-day mood diary, and a review of beta session logs.

03Framework

An Adaptive Tone Framework

With ML engineers and the PM, I designed a framework that intercepts each entry to read the room first. It maps input to four emotional states using LLM context analysis, not keyword matching, then picks a tone and a response pace.

04Design

Designing My Journal

Once the framework existed, I had to make it felt in the interface. I led the redesign of the journal screen around two decisions that make the AI read as a companion rather than a machine.

Decision 01
A thinking indicator with variable latency

Instant replies felt robotic, even with the right tone. I chose typing dots over a generic "Generating response…" and tied the delay to how heavy the entry is, capped at 3 seconds.

A · GenericGenerating response…B · Selected
Light check-in1–1.5s
Moderate sharing1.5–2s
Heavy emotional2–3s
Decision 02
Training wheels for the blank page

Contextual starters help first-run users begin, then fade once journaling becomes self-directed. An on-demand help button stays as a safety net.

05Alignment

Turning conflict into co-creation

The hardest part wasn't the pixels. It was getting the team to agree on why we were building it this way. I acted as the bridge: reframing technical debates through user psychology and building shared artifacts that gave every discipline ownership of the solution.

Debate 01 · with Engineering
Speed vs. care
Lead engineer

Me

Variant · winner1.5–3sVariable latency. Higher "feeling heard," zero "slow" complaints

Debate 02 · with the PM
Friction vs. data
PM

"Users will freeze and churn without prompts."

Me

"Button-mashing prompts give us garbage data."

Compromise: the training-wheels pattern. Retention protected for the PM, data quality intact for design.

07Reflection

What I learned

Good product design is about designing relationships.

01
Conflict into collaboration

I aligned Engineering's need for logic with design's need for empathy by building a framework both could contribute to.

02
Rituals over features

Success came from refining the daily ritual of journaling to feel emotionally safe. A smaller, warmer feature set beat a larger, colder one.

03
AI that reads the room

The best AI products don't just process language. They understand context, respect emotion and make space for vulnerability.
