01Context

Users were dropping off, but why?

TAP3D is a B2B VR training platform preparing workers for advanced manufacturing roles, with one experience across VR and web. Despite that, internal data showed a significant lag in the training completion cycle. Trainees weren't reaching the review stage, and that hit customer retention directly.

ExpectedComplete training
→
GoalHigher retention
≠
RealityEarly drop-off

The logs showed where trainees stopped. They couldn't show why.

04Design

Designing the agent

Before testing anything, I defined what the agent listens to, when it's allowed to speak, and where a human takes over. This blueprint became the shared spec for engineering and the script I followed as the "AI" in the Wizard-of-Oz study.

One component, three behaviors, three surfaces, and a human for anything the agent can't solve

Decision 01
One next step, not a list

Trainees already had a list and ignored it. The agent always proposes exactly one task, with its time cost, so starting feels small.

CNC ProgrammingEquipment Lockout quizFluid Dynamics intro Leak Checking · 10 min
Decision 02
Hand off to a human, don't fake an answer

Equipment errors in a simulation are high stakes. The agent packages the context, and the instructor stays the one who answers.

Traineeuploads clip→AIdrafts context→Instructorreplies
Decision 03
One widget, three surfaces

I designed the agent as a single modular component so it reads the same in a headset, on a laptop and on a tablet, and joined the design system instead of living on one screen.

05Validation

Simulating AI to prove ROI

The checklist made tasks visible, but it still waited for trainees to act. Three questions stayed unanswered.

MVP · ChecklistPhase 1
  • See task listsolved
  • "What should I do first?"unsolved
  • "I'm stuck, need help now"unsolved
  • "What did I do last week?"unsolved
Proposed · AI layerPhase 2
  • See task list✓
  • Personalized "do this now"✓
  • Context-aware issue handoff✓
  • Weekly progress summary✓

To convince skeptical stakeholders, I ran a Wizard-of-Oz simulation with 24 trainees and acted as the AI backend in real time.

Trainees saw an agent. Behind the curtain, I answered with templates and live signals, following the blueprint's rules.

Issue resolution
33%→71%
+38pt with context-aware handoff
Task start rate
38%→63%
+25pt with "do this now" nudges

How we measured · 24 trainees · checklist MVP vs. simulated agent behaviors. A human played the AI, so these numbers show demand for the behavior, not model accuracy.

06Outcome

The CFO said yes

I put the AI agent screens next to the MVP screens for engineering, the PM and the CFO. The CFO was worried about ROI and development cost, and the simulation numbers answered it. We rolled the agent out right after the VR AI integration wrapped.

Post-rollout completion metrics fall outside my engagement window. The validation data above is what secured the investment.

07Reflection

What I'd carry forward

Pre-launch simulation testing wasn't common at TAP3D. This project made it part of how the team decides what to build.
