01Problem

12 of 12 cooks broke flow to interact

Recipe video apps assume clean, dry fingers. In reality cooks are elbow-deep in flour, raw meat or wet ingredients. Every interaction means wash → dry → tap → resume, adding 12–18 seconds per step. In our diary study, the most-logged frustration was "wanted to tap but couldn't." 9 of the 12 cooks had already invented elbow taps.

An audit of leading recipe apps (NYT Cooking, Tasty, Paprika) confirmed the gap: every interaction model assumed clean hands and a quiet room.

07Reflection

What I learned

The breakthrough wasn't adding input modalities. It was listening to what was already failing.

Old approachAdd more

Stack voice, gesture, gaze and touch and hope one works. Each layer adds latency, false positives and confusion.

New approachRead failure

Failure signals are the input. Two failed modalities plus proximity means the system already knows what to do. Zero new learning curve.

Context is the best input

Body mechanics drive digital design
