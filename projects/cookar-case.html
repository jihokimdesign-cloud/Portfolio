01Who it's for

Designed for people with low vision

Low vision isn't the same as blindness. Many people with low vision cook using the sight they have, but small, low-contrast parts are hard to tell apart: which end of a knife to grab, where a pan's handle is, which surface is hot.

CookAR works with that remaining sight. It adds affordance augmentations, high-contrast visual cues that separate the part you grab from the part that can hurt you.

02System

From camera to cue in real time

The system pairs a fine-tuned affordance segmentation model with a stereo-camera AR headset, generating visual augmentations directly on the tools a user is reaching for.

03Study

Shaped by low-vision participants

To train the model, the team collected and annotated the first egocentric dataset of kitchen tool affordances. Then the augmentation designs were refined through a qualitative lab study with ten low-vision participants, so the cues reflect what they could actually see and use.
